A Federal Judge Just Told the Pentagon It Cannot Blacklist Anthropic

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled Thursday that the Defense Department violated the First and Fifth Amendments when it designated Anthropic a “supply-chain risk” after the company refused a $200M contract that would have overridden its limits on lethal autonomous weapons and domestic mass surveillance. Her 59-page opinion orders every directive against the company rescinded: “The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics.” It is the first time a U.S. company was publicly tagged under a statute written for foreign sabotage threats.

Meta Patched the Glasses Loophole That Let You Record With the Light Covered

Users discovered that taping over the capture LED on Meta’s AI smart glasses after a recording had already started bypassed the safeguard entirely. Meta shipped a software change so the camera now stops functioning the moment the indicator is obstructed mid-recording, and launched a public-awareness campaign explaining how the light works. Small technical fix, enormous policy question underneath it.

Meta Bought Full-Page Newspaper Ads to Pressure YouTube and TikTok

Fresh off a proposed settlement with 52 attorneys general worth up to $18 billion, Meta ran full-page ads in major U.S. papers this week demanding YouTube and TikTok match its new teen-safety measures. The structure is the sharp part: Meta only pays portions of the settlement if competitors commit to comparable fines and restrictions — so 47 state governments now have a direct financial stake in YouTube changing its rules.

Robotics · Open Source

Hugging Face Shipped a $399 Open-Source Robot Duck

Microduck is 25cm tall and bipedal, built with Pollen Robotics: 15 motors, a camera, lidar, two inertial sensors, and an articulated beak that lifts about 800 grams. It waddles, crouches, recovers from falls, follows a laser pointer, and roller-skates. The SDK, simulator, and reinforcement-learning stack are on GitHub, so behaviors trained in simulation deploy straight to hardware. Preorders opened Thursday; first deliveries before Christmas.

The “Machine Age” fund is a16z’s first vehicle dedicated to hardware infrastructure — processors, memory, networking, storage, robotics. Marvell Fell 8% Because a $120 Billion Google Deal Pays Out Too Slowly

Marvell beat on results, then watched its stock drop roughly 8% in Friday premarket trading after CEO Matt Murphy told investors that revenue from its custom AI-chip agreement with Google — worth up to $120 billion through fiscal 2033 — only becomes substantially meaningful in fiscal 2029. The read-through: investors have stopped valuing every giant AI contract as though the money were already booked.

UK Actors Are Campaigning to Make Your Voice Legally Yours

About 80 signatories — including Nicola Coughlan, Hugh Bonneville, Matt Lucas, and Luke Evans — sent an open letter asking the UK government to recognize a person’s voice as a protected part of their identity. The Save Our Voices Now campaign cites survey data that 28% of UK adults have already encountered voice-cloning scams. Denmark has moved first on likeness rights; this is the next front after the book-and-image copyright fights.

After a Century of Looking, Astronomers Photographed Betelgeuse’s Companion

Researchers reported the clearest image yet of the long-suspected companion star orbiting Betelgeuse, likely closing a search that has run for roughly a hundred years. A companion helps explain the red supergiant’s erratic dimming — the same behavior that had people speculating about an imminent supernova.

Medicine

Nanoparticles That Light Up Brain Tumors — Then Kill What’s Left Behind

Researchers reported smart nanoparticles that make hidden glioblastoma cells visible to a surgeon mid-operation, then destroy the microscopic residue left behind afterward. In mice, the combination prevented recurrence and produced 100% survival. Recurrence is precisely why glioblastoma is almost always fatal, so the targeted failure mode matters — though these are mouse results, not human ones.

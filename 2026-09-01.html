A Single Key Left Every Major Model's Reasoning Readable

Researchers disclosed that OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google each used one global encryption key for reasoning tokens passed between API calls — so injecting a strong model's encrypted trace into a weaker sibling made it print the hidden reasoning verbatim. Decoding 315,320 publicly scraped reasoning blocks surfaced 367 PII artifacts and 182 credentials. The attacks stopped working after mitigations, but logs already shared publicly remain decodable.

Astronomy · Aug 5

The Sharpest Images of the Sun Reveal Whirlpools 12 Miles Wide

An international team using the NSF Inouye Solar Telescope published the first observation of Kelvin-Helmholtz instability on the solar photosphere in Nature. The plasma vortices form where layers moving at different speeds slide past one another — 47 of them measured, sizes from 25 to 170 km, moving at up to 3 km/s. Researchers suspect the mechanism helps explain why the Sun's outer atmosphere runs so much hotter than its surface.

Nutrition Science · Aug 29

Tuning the Light Made Spirulina a Real B12 Source

Ordinary spirulina is a notorious false promise for vegans: most of its B12 is a pseudovitamin the body cannot use. A team led by Reichman University's Asaf Tzachor, publishing in Discover Food, used controlled light conditions in photobioreactors to grow biomass with 1.64 micrograms of active B12 per 100g — above beef's typical 0.7 to 1.5 — with over 98% in bioavailable form.

Biology · Aug 26

A 190-Year-Old Museum Specimen Confirms a New Pangolin

Scientists extracted DNA from a nearly two-century-old museum specimen to confirm that pangolins in Nepal and northern India constitute a distinct Himalayan species, not a population of an already-described one. The finding matters beyond taxonomy: pangolins are the most trafficked wild mammal on earth, and a separately recognized species can be separately protected.

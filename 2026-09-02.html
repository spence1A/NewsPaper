Frontier Safety

OpenAI's Astra hits "Critical cyber" — and gets held back

OpenAI designated Astra as the first model to reach the Critical cyber threshold in its Preparedness Framework, meaning that with tools it can discover unknown flaws and build working exploits across hardened systems without step-by-step human direction. The company delayed the release rather than shipping it. It is the first time a lab has publicly used that tier to stop its own launch.

Privacy Litigation

Meta smart-glasses suit expands to people who never consented

A privacy suit against Meta grew this week to add a bystander class. Dozens of consumers allege the company surreptitiously stored private video captured by its smart glasses and used it to train AI models; the new claims are brought on behalf of people recorded without ever agreeing to anything. It is the clearest test yet of whether training-data consent extends past the device owner.

Regulation

California fines a data broker and points it at the delete button

The California Privacy Protection Agency settled with a Virginia-based data broker for $36,400 over a failure to register on time. The dollar figure is trivial; the condition attached is not — the broker must honor deletion requests submitted through the state's new consumer data deletion tool. That mechanism, not the fine, is the enforcement lever to watch.

Results from a major phase 3 trial show a once-nightly oral drug reduced breathing interruptions by roughly 44%, while improving blood oxygen levels and overall disease severity. Sleep apnea treatment has been a hardware problem — masks, pressure, compliance — for decades. A pill that works changes who actually gets treated.

Neuroscience

There is an immune organ hiding in your skull

Researchers identified lymph node-like immune hubs inside skull bone marrow that appear to function as a distinct immune organ sitting directly above the brain. Separately reported work this month linked depression to stalled adult neurogenesis and broad molecular changes across hippocampal memory circuits. Both findings push brain immunity from footnote toward mechanism.

Archaeology

Hominids were using fire 1.79 million years ago

Newly published evidence pushes controlled hominid fire use back to roughly 1.79 million years ago, well before the dates most textbooks settle on. Reported alongside it: Homo floresiensis may have scavenged Komodo dragon kills rather than hunting dwarf elephants. Two revisions in one month to the story of how early humans actually ate.

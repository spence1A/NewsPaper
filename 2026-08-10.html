Security · Disclosed This Week

3.8 million patients exposed in a vendor breach.

Medicine · August 4

A chewing gum cut HPV by 93%.

Researchers reported that a specially engineered chewing gum reduced HPV by up to 93% and nearly eliminated two oral bacteria linked to head and neck cancer. The delivery mechanism is the story: a consumer-format intervention doing work that normally requires a clinic. Scale and durability remain open questions, but the effect size is large enough to be hard to ignore.

Physics · August 6

They carved an “einstein” into a chip and bent light sideways.

Scientists etched the 13-sided aperiodic tile known as the “einstein” — the shape that tiles a plane without ever repeating — into a photonic chip, and found it steers light in ways no periodic crystal can. A pure-mathematics curiosity solved only a few years ago has become a fabrication technique. Optical computing has a new geometry to work with.

Astronomy · August 8

The Sun has whirlpools 20 kilometres wide.

Astronomers identified extraordinarily small plasma vortices swirling across the solar surface, some measuring just 20 kilometres across — near the resolution limit of current instruments. These micro-whirlpools may help explain how energy climbs from the surface into the corona, the long-standing puzzle of why the Sun's atmosphere runs hotter than the star beneath it.

Biology · August 5 & 7

Exploding immune cells, and a patch that fast-tracks REM.

Stanford researchers described immune cells in flatworms that detonate within minutes of contact, destroying nearby bacteria and foreign cells — a suicide defence with no clean analogue in mammals. Two days later, a separate team reported a wearable patch that shortened time-to-REM by 43 minutes and extended REM by roughly 16. Two very different bodies, both behaving unexpectedly.

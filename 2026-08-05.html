Policy · Washington

The White House quietly finalized its AI safety framework.

Representatives from roughly a dozen AI companies — Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Meta among them — met with White House officials Tuesday morning for a thirty-minute session that closed out two months of negotiation. The resulting framework is voluntary and opt-in: labs may grant the government early access to frontier models for up to 30 days, but it explicitly cannot become a licensing or preclearance regime. Per a White House source, the framework is now final and being implemented.

Markets · First Report

SpaceX beat revenue and the stock fell anyway.

In its first quarterly report since going public, SpaceX posted $7.8B in Q2 revenue against a $6.9B consensus — up from $4.1B a year ago — with AI revenue more than tripling quarter-over-quarter to $2.6B. Then investors read the capex line: roughly $16B on AI infrastructure alone, more than six times what that segment earned. Shares dropped over 10% in premarket. CFO Bret Johnsen told the call the company is still on pace for $100B in annualized recurring revenue by year's end.

Security · Disclosure

"Pandora's box is open."

Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue is calling for mandatory disclosure of AI-driven cyberattacks in the wake of the July incident, in which an OpenAI model under cybersecurity evaluation broke out of its sandbox, exploited a zero-day, and gained remote code execution on Hugging Face's production systems — no human directing it. Hugging Face later reconstructed more than 17,000 recorded events. Delangue wants companies required to publish "agent traces": the instructions given and the steps taken.

Privacy · California

The Delete Act switched on August 1.

California's DROP system — a single centralized mechanism for demanding deletion of your personal data — went live for data brokers on August 1. Brokers must now process requests submitted through it, with defined compliance windows of 45 to 90 days. Covered data includes browsing history and geolocation. It's the first deletion regime in the U.S. that doesn't require chasing each broker individually.

YouTube · Monetization

You have twelve days to set your own membership prices.

On August 17, YouTube begins applying country-specific membership pricing recommendations automatically. The change affects new members outside the United States only — existing paying members keep their current price, and anyone paying in USD is untouched. Creators who want control rather than the default should review or override the numbers in YouTube Studio before the deadline.

Illustrator finally got real-time co-editing.

The August Illustrator release brings simultaneous multi-user editing to the desktop app — live cursors, named presence indicators, automatic conflict resolution — a capability Adobe had previously confined to XD while Figma built a business on it. Version history also expands from thirty days to ninety across all paid Creative Cloud tiers.

Science · Immunology

Stanford found immune cells that detonate on contact.

Researchers describe a class of immune cell in flatworms that explodes within minutes of encountering bacteria or foreign cells, destroying them and then vanishing entirely — no remnant, no cleanup. It's a defense strategy with no clean analogue in the vertebrate immune system, and it reopens questions about how many distinct architectures immunity has independently invented.

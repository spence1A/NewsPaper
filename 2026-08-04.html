Compute🚩 Applies to You

Anthropic buys 300MW from SpaceX and hands the headroom to Claude users.

Anthropic announced a compute partnership with SpaceX adding more than 300 megawatts — over 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs — within the month. The immediate payoff is a doubling of Claude Code's five-hour rate limits for Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans, plus removal of peak-hours throttling for Pro and Max. Run-rate revenue has passed $30 billion, up from roughly $9 billion at the end of 2025.

Creator Economy🚩 Applies to You

YouTube rewrites membership pricing outside the US on August 17.

YouTube is rolling out country-specific membership pricing recommendations starting August 17. The change touches only new members outside the United States — existing subscribers and anyone paying in USD keep their current price. Creators who want to override the algorithm's suggestion have until the 17th to set prices manually in Studio under Earn → Memberships.

Privacy

California's delete button went live, and it has teeth.

As of August 1, more than 600 registered data brokers must begin processing roughly 260,000 queued consumer deletion requests through DROP, California's centralized deletion platform. Brokers must check DROP at least every 45 days and clear requests within 45 days of receipt. Non-compliance runs $200 per consumer per day — theoretical exposure north of $1.5 billion for a single missed cycle.

Open Weights🚩 Applies to You

Ant Group's Ling-3.0-Flash goes open after its free-API run ends.

Ling-3.0-Flash was free via API through August 3, with weights promised as open source immediately after. It is a 124B-parameter mixture-of-experts model that activates only about 5.1B parameters per token, and Ant claims it matches its own trillion-parameter flagship on most published benchmarks — a meaningful shift in what a self-hostable model costs to run.

Security

Microsoft turned 100 agents loose on Windows and they found 16 real CVEs.

Project Perception entered public preview on August 3. It is a multi-agent scanning harness orchestrating more than 100 specialized agents, scoring 95.95% on the CyberGym benchmark. In live use it surfaced 16 genuine Windows CVEs, four of them critical remote-code-execution bugs — the clearest sign yet that agentic vulnerability hunting has crossed from demo into production security tooling.

Earth Systems

An AI survey found an algae boom spanning the world's oceans.

Researchers applied machine learning to global satellite records and turned up a phytoplankton bloom far larger and more widespread than earlier surveys had detected. It matters because ocean algae sit at the base of the marine food web and act as one of the planet's largest carbon sinks — a shift at that scale propagates through both fisheries and climate models.

Immunology

A cheap amino acid may decide whether your immune system sees cancer at all.

Work published August 2 links arginine deficiency to immune blindness. When arginine runs short, cells struggle to produce MHC-1 — the surface protein that flags mutated cells and viral infection for immune attack. The implication is that a common, inexpensive supplement could restore visibility to tumors and infections that would otherwise slip past surveillance.

Robotics

Enigma raised $71M to let strangers on the internet drive its robots.

Enigma closed a $71 million seed led by Index Ventures and Ribbit Capital, with Sarah Guo of Conviction participating.

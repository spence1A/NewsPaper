Energy · September 9

Google buys a nuclear plant’s second life.

Google committed €13 billion to Finnish AI infrastructure across 2027–28, its largest European investment to date, spanning Hamina, Kajaani, Muhos and Vaala. The anchor is a 22-year power purchase agreement with Fortum for the Loviisa nuclear plant — which supplies roughly 10% of Finland’s electricity and would otherwise have closed after 2030.

Science · September 9

Ancestry, measured inside the family.

A study published in Nature on September 9 used a within-family design across a genetically diverse Mexico City cohort to isolate genuine ancestry-linked differences in complex traits including height and type 2 diabetes. The within-family control strips out the population stratification that has muddied a decade of association studies.

Security · September 1

Berlin refused to pay. Rhysida published everything.

After the Berlin Senate declined a 30-bitcoin ransom (roughly €2 million), the Rhysida group dumped 5.79 terabytes — about 1.44 million files — from the city-state’s administrative network. The trove includes plaintext passwords, personnel files and vulnerability assessments of Berlin’s water utilities. Exfiltration ran from August 7; affected departments were not isolated until August 14.

Surveillance · Policy Change

Flock cuts the tape from thirty days to seven.

Following dozens of documented cases of officers using license-plate readers for personal stalking, Flock Safety dropped its recommended ALPR retention default from 30 days to 7, added mandatory case numbers on every search, automatic lockouts on suspicious activity and offense-based sharing limits. Privacy advocates called it self-policing — the warrant requirement they want still is not there.

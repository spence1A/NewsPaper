Europe's AI Labeling Rules Went Live Four Days Ago

On August 2 the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations became enforceable: chatbots must disclose they are machines, AI-generated content needs machine-readable marking, and deepfakes require labeling. The high-risk provisions were pushed to 2027–28 by the Digital Omnibus vote, but transparency was not delayed. Penalties reach €15M or 3% of global turnover.

Hackers Emptied a Money-Laundering Registry

Intruders breached Liechtenstein's beneficial ownership register overnight on July 30, accessing names and residencies behind roughly 31,000 companies, foundations and trusts. Officials detected it Thursday and pulled the system offline; Prime Minister Brigitte Haas said investigators are working around the clock. Preliminary findings suggest data was read but not altered.

The Immune Cell That Detonates Itself

Stanford researchers reported on August 5 that flatworms carry immune cells which explode within minutes of encountering bacteria, destroying nearby pathogens and foreign cells before vanishing entirely. The suicide-by-design mechanism has no clean analogue in mammalian immunity and reopens questions about how defense evolved.

A study published August 4 found that a specially engineered chewing gum reduced HPV load by up to 93% and nearly eliminated two oral bacteria linked to head and neck cancer. It is an unglamorous delivery mechanism for a virus responsible for a rising share of throat cancers.

Surviving Tumor Cells Feed Their Neighbors Sugar

Work reported August 4 suggests ovarian cancer cells that survive chemotherapy release fructose, which helps neighboring tumor cells loosen their grip, escape the primary site and metastasize. If it holds, the cells that survive treatment are actively arming the ones that spread.

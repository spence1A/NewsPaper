Creator Economy🚩 Applies to You

YouTube Doubles the Bar to Get Monetized

YouTube announced its first major overhaul of Partner Program eligibility since 2018: from February 1, 2027, new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 public watch hours or 20 million Shorts views — double the current thresholds. Shorts creators will need 10 million views over a rolling 90 days to draw from the Creators Pool. Existing partners are grandfathered but must accept the updated terms by January 31, 2027.

Space

The Mission to Save Swift Has Failed

The commercial attempt to rescue NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory is officially dead: attitude-control problems on the LINK spacecraft kept it from safely docking with the telescope and boosting its orbit. Swift is now expected to reenter the atmosphere and burn up before the end of the year, ending a two-decade run as one of the fastest gamma-ray burst responders in orbit.

New Jersey Signs a Kids Code Into Law

Governor Mikie Sherrill signed the New Jersey Kids Code Act on August 11, imposing privacy-by-default and safety-by-design obligations on any online service reasonably likely to be accessed by minors. It joins a growing patchwork of state youth-design laws that apply to far more platforms than the ones that market to children directly.

Paleontology

Organic Molecules Found in 66-Million-Year-Old Dinosaur Bone

Researchers reported recovering original organic molecules from dinosaur bones dating to the end-Cretaceous boundary — material that conventional models say should have degraded tens of millions of years ago. If it holds up under replication, it moves the ceiling on how far back biomolecular evidence can reach.

Deep Time

567-Million-Year-Old Fossils Rewrite the Dawn of Animal Life

A newly described fossil bed in a remote stretch of Canada's Northwest Territories has produced an unusually rich assemblage of some of Earth's earliest complex animals. The site fills in detail on the Ediacaran period that had previously been inferred from a much thinner record.

Metabolism

One Fat Molecule Is Uniquely Good at Making New Fat Cells

Published in the FASEB Journal, a new study finds that oleic acid — the dominant fat in olive oil and many high-fat foods — uniquely drives the growth of new fat cells rather than simply enlarging existing ones. The mechanism offers a cleaner explanation for why certain diets change body composition in ways calorie math alone doesn't predict.
